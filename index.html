Document Authority Hierarchy

This "Order of Precedence" determines which rules take priority. Higher levels override lower levels in the event of a conflict.

Level 1: Federal & State Law (Supreme Authority)
National & State Statutes

All community rules must comply with the U.S. Constitution and Florida Statute Chapter 720. State law protects rights that an HOA cannot ban, such as flying the American flag or displaying specific religious symbols.

Level 2: The Community Declaration (CC&Rs)
The Neighborhood "Constitution"

The Master Declaration is the primary local document. It establishes property rights, easements, assessment obligations, and permanent restrictions recorded with the county.

Level 3: Articles & Bylaws
Corporate & Administrative Law

The Articles of Incorporation create the legal entity. The Bylaws serve as the "Operations Manual" for Board elections, meeting notices, and voting requirements.

Level 4: Rules, Regulations & Design Guidelines
Board-Adopted Specifics

These provide the most detailed day-to-day rules, including Architectural (ARC) standards for fences and landscaping, as well as amenity usage policies.

Common Resident Questions

Click a topic below to see its context. You can ask the AI Assistant for more details on any of these inquiries.

🏠 Architectural Changes & Fences

Common Question: "Can I install a fence, and what materials are allowed?"
Governing documents specify that wood and chain-link fences are prohibited. Almost all exterior home modifications require prior approval from the Architectural Review Committee (ARC).

🐾 Pets & Local Wildlife

Common Question: "Are there restrictions on the number of pets or feeding wildlife?"
The documents limit the number of household pets and prohibit commercial breeding. To maintain community safety, feeding wild animals (such as alligators) is strictly prohibited.

🚗 Golf Carts & Parking

Common Question: "Where are my guests allowed to park?"
Street parking is restricted to specific timeframes. Golf carts must meet safety standards (mirrors/lights) to be operated on community paths and roads.

🎂 Age Restrictions (55+ Community)

Common Question: "What is the minimum age for residents?"
As an age-qualified community, at least 80% of occupied homes must have one resident aged 55 or older. Permanent residents must be at least 22 years of age.

🗳️ Meetings, Voting & Quorums

Common Question: "How many people need to vote for an election to count?"
The Bylaws define the 'Quorum' requirement—the minimum number of voting members who must participate for an election or vote to be legally valid.
